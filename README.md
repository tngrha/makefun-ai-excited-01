At dusk over a glowing city, a vast murmuration of starlings sweeps across
the sky — and the flock itself becomes a giant phoenix, wings spread wide,
ember light catching the edges of thousands of birds. Far below, one tiny
child stands on a rooftop and reaches up toward it.

It excites because of the double take: at first glance it reads as fire,
a creature of flame rising over the skyline; on a second look it is alive,
made of birds. The diagonal sweep of the flock gives it momentum, the tiny
child against the enormous phoenix gives it scale and awe, and the warm
windows and individual birds reward a closer look.

## The concept

The idea is a surprise built out of scale. A murmuration is already one
of nature's most exciting sights — thousands of birds moving like a single
liquid animal. Here the flock takes the shape of a phoenix, the mythical
bird of fire and rebirth, so the sky itself seems to catch flame. The
child on the rooftop is the viewer inside the picture: small, still, and
reaching up, the way you want to reach up when something impossible
happens overhead. Nothing in the scene is a stock explosion or firework;
the wonder comes from something real — birds — arranged into something
mythical.
