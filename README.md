# Murmuration Phoenix

A painterly dusk sky over a city skyline, where a vast murmuration of
thousands of starlings sweeps diagonally across the indigo dark — and the
flock itself becomes a giant phoenix, wings spread wide, ember-orange
light catching the birds' edges. Far below, one tiny child stands on a
rooftop, arm raised toward the impossible bird. The city's windows glow
warm gold beneath it all.

At first glance it reads as fire. A second look reveals it is alive.
That double-take is the whole point: the jolt of awe comes first, the
surprise lands a beat later, and the diagonal sweep of the flock keeps
your eye moving the way the birds do. Look closer still and the detail
rewards you — individual birds, lit windows, the smallness of the child
against the scale of the sky.

## The concept

The idea started from a simple question: what is the most exciting thing
that could happen in an ordinary city sky, on an ordinary evening? Not an
explosion — those are stock. A murmuration is real, it happens every dusk,
and for a few seconds thousands of birds can look like one enormous
creature. So the image leans into that: the phoenix is not drawn, it is
flocked into being. The fire you think you see is birds catching the last
light. And the child on the roof is the viewer — small, still, arm raised,
the only person in the city who noticed. Excitement, for this image, is
not noise; it is the moment something impossible turns out to be alive.

## Run

```sh
MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=/output node main.ts
```

This copies `entry.png` into the output directory (creating it if needed).
`main.ts` uses only the Node standard library and first checks that the
image is a PNG or JPEG within the competition gate (at most 1024x1024
pixels, at most 1 MiB).
